# Concepts: A night-market vendor chases a monkey that stole her late grandmother's recipe tin and learns why it took it

Emotional core of the idea: grief that has been sealed shut (the tin she can't open) meets someone else's grief that has not. The monkey's motive should turn the chase from "get my thing back" into "I am not the only one who misses her."
Runtime/medium: 90 s, photoreal live action, Veo 3.1 clips of 4-8 s. Sized as 2 main characters (1 human, 1 animal), 2 locations, 3 scenes. These concepts are not canon.

## C1 — The Two Knocks
- **Logline:** A grieving noodle vendor chases a macaque that snatches her late grandmother's recipe tin, and finds it carrying the tin to the spot where her grandmother secretly fed it every night.
- **Hook:** A monkey grabs the one object the heroine is too afraid to open, and bolts through a lantern-lit crowd.
- **Engine:** Want: the tin back. Obstacle: a fast animal in a dense market. Cannot be avoided: the tin is the last thing of the grandmother's she has.
- **Central question:** Will she take the tin back, or finally open it and carry on what it was for?
- **Ending direction:** Bittersweet, warm. She keeps her grandmother's nightly ritual alive.
- **Twist potential:** Motive twist. The monkey is not stealing; it is answering a signal (two knocks on the tin lid) that only her grandmother used. Clues: worn bright spots on the lid, a chipped saucer she keeps nudging aside, the monkey watching the stall nightly.
- **Producibility:** 2 characters, 2 locations (stall in market, quiet alley). Risk: crowd chase (keep crowd soft-focus), hands tapping the tin.

## C2 — The Collector
- **Logline:** A vendor chases a monkey through the night market and discovers its hoard of lost things, including the grandmother's missing ladle, the one tool her recipe never worked without.
- **Hook:** A heist-comedy chase that ends in a treasure room of lost objects.
- **Engine:** Want: the tin back. Obstacle: the monkey's tricks and a hoard guarded by it. Unavoidable because the recipe fails without the ladle.
- **Central question:** Can she get both the tin and the ladle back?
- **Ending direction:** Light, comic, satisfying.
- **Twist potential:** The monkey "returns" things rather than steals. Clue: small objects reappear at stalls.
- **Producibility:** Many small props to keep consistent across shots; a hoard set is expensive. Higher risk.

## C3 — Half a Recipe
- **Logline:** A vendor chases a monkey owned by her rival, and learns the rival's late grandmother and her own were partners, and the monkey carries the missing half of the recipe.
- **Hook:** Rivalry story where the thief turns out to be a delivery.
- **Engine:** Want: stop the rival. Obstacle: the rival's monkey. Unavoidable: the rival stands to win the market's contest.
- **Central question:** Will the two vendors share what their grandmothers started?
- **Ending direction:** Reconciliation.
- **Twist potential:** Motive twist via the rival. Needs a third character and dialogue to explain.
- **Producibility:** 3 characters, text-heavy reveal (handwritten recipe). Dialogue-dependent, weak for 90 s.

## C4 — Festival of Lanterns
- **Logline:** On the night the dead are said to visit, a vendor chases a monkey carrying her grandmother's tin toward a small altar and meets a final taste of her grandmother's cooking.
- **Hook:** Chase turns magical in the last act.
- **Engine:** Want: the tin back. Obstacle: the chase. Unavoidable: it is the festival night.
- **Central question:** Is her grandmother still near?
- **Ending direction:** Mystical, ambiguous.
- **Twist potential:** Supernatural reveal. Weaker fairness: the rules appear only at the end.
- **Producibility:** Spirit effects are hard to keep photoreal; religious specifics need care.

## Recommendation
C1. It has the clearest reveal, the cleanest setups and payoffs (all visible, no dialogue needed), and it is the most producible for Veo: 2 characters, 2 locations, no text on screen needed. C2 is the best fallback if you want comedy.
