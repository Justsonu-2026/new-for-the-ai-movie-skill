## 1. Mix priorities
- Dialogue first in most moments; carve space for it (lower music mid-range, duck ambience slightly).
- One focal sound at a time at key beats (the turn of a scene should not be crowded).
- Dynamics: contrast loud and quiet; a drop before a big moment makes it bigger.
- Consistency: ambience levels match across cuts within a scene.

## 2. Stems and M&E
Deliver separate stems: **Dialogue**, **Music**, **Effects** (Foley + hard + designed + ambience), plus a full
mix. An **M&E** track (music and effects, no dialogue) lets the film be dubbed into other languages; make
sure effects recorded with dialogue (cloth, breaths, footsteps) also exist on the effects stem.

## 3. Loudness targets
Commonly cited integrated-loudness targets (verify with the platform's current delivery spec before final):
- Broadcast Europe, EBU R128: −23 LUFS.
- Broadcast US, ATSC A/85: −24 LKFS.
- Online video platforms normalize playback; mixes for them are often delivered louder (around −14 to −16
  LUFS is commonly used) — check the platform.
- Streaming services with professional delivery specs publish their own (often dialogue-gated) targets.
Also keep true peak below the spec's ceiling (often −1 or −2 dBTP).

## 4. SFX generation prompts
Describe: source object and material, action, intensity, space and distance, duration, and what is not in
the sound. Example: "Close-up Foley of wet canvas sneakers slapping a flat tar rooftop, three quick running
steps, splashes in a shallow puddle on the third, outdoor with light rain around, dry and close, no music,
no voices, 1.5 seconds." Generate variations; pick by the checklist.

## 5. Review checklist
- [ ] Right sound for the source and material
- [ ] In sync with the picture event (± 1 frame for hard sync)
- [ ] Perspective matches the shot size
- [ ] No artifacts (warble, clicks, loop seams, generated voices in an effect)
- [ ] Ambience continuous within the scene
- [ ] Dialogue intelligible over everything
- [ ] Loudness and true peak within target (measure; don't guess)
