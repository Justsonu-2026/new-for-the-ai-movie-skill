## 1. Layers
| Layer | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogue | voices (voice-director) | the narrative spine; protect intelligibility |
| Foley | footsteps, cloth movement, props handled | performed in sync; material-specific |
| Hard effects | sync sounds of objects/events (door, car, glass) | from libraries or generation |
| Designed effects | created sounds (impacts, whooshes, creatures, magic) | heightened; use sparingly |
| Ambience / backgrounds | the place's continuous sound | glues shots into one space |
| Room tone | the quiet sound of an interior | needed under dialogue edits |
| Music | music-director | shares space with effects |

## 2. Spotting a shot
For each shot list: actions that make sound (with sync time), surfaces and materials, the ambience
continuing under it, off-screen elements, and the transition sound into the next shot. Example:
`SC02-SH060 2.5 s: wet sneakers slap tar ×3 (Foley, close); 3.4 s: jacket flap (Foley); 3.6 s: leap — breath
effort (dialogue/voice-director); ambience: heavy rain bed + neon buzz (L2 sign, frame right).`

## 3. Perspective and space
Match sound distance to the shot: close-ups get detailed close sounds; wide shots get distant, more
reverberant versions. Interiors have reflections matching their size and materials (tiled stairwell =
bright echo; carpeted room = dead). Perspective changes with every cut, but ambience continuity holds the
scene together.

## 4. Off-screen sound
Sound expands the world beyond the frame cheaply: traffic, neighbours, weather, a distant train. It can
also plant story (footsteps on the stairs before the pursuer appears). Off-screen sound is a strong tool in
AI films because it adds world without extra generated imagery.

## 5. Sound bridges and transitions
- **J-cut:** the next scene's sound starts before its picture (pulls the audience forward).
- **L-cut:** the current scene's sound continues over the next picture (lets a moment linger).
- **Hard cut in sound:** contrast (loud chase → silent room).
- **Matching sounds:** a sound that transforms into another across the cut (a scream into a kettle).

## 6. Silence
Silence is relative: drop the ambience to near nothing, leave one small sound (a clock, a breath). Use before
scares, at revelations, after losses. Plan it with the music-director so music doesn't fill it.

## 7. Genre notes
- **Horror:** low drones, sparse ambience, silence before scares, sounds from off-screen.
- **Comedy:** crisp, slightly exaggerated Foley; timing of the sound is part of the joke.
- **Action:** layered impacts (transient + body + tail), whooshes on fast moves, dynamic contrast (drop out
  before the big hit).
- **Drama:** naturalistic, intimate Foley, room tone, restraint.
- **Romance:** soft ambience, breath, cloth, room for music.
