## 1. Diagnosis checklist
Mark each with the scene/beat where it occurs.
- [ ] Opening: does the first 10% establish the protagonist, their want, and a question?
- [ ] Inciting incident arrives early enough (shorts: within the first ~20% of runtime).
- [ ] Protagonist is active: they make choices that cause events, not just react.
- [ ] Want is clear by the end of the first act/beat; stakes are personal.
- [ ] Every scene turns a value (start ≠ end).
- [ ] No two scenes do the same job (repeated beats).
- [ ] Exposition is dramatized, not dumped.
- [ ] Big moments get a reaction beat (the audience needs time to feel).
- [ ] Climax is the biggest moment and depends on the protagonist's choice.
- [ ] Ending pays off setups and answers the central question.
- [ ] Nothing important happens off screen that the audience needed to see.
- [ ] Tone is consistent, or shifts are deliberate.

## 2. Keep / cut / merge / add / change rules
- **Keep** anything that works, even if rough; polish it rather than replace it.
- **Cut** a scene that doesn't turn, repeats an earlier turn, or explains what we already know. Also cut
  characters who don't change anything; give their function to another character.
- **Merge** two scenes in the same place or with the same function into one stronger scene.
- **Add** only to fix a real gap: a missing motivation, a missing setup for a payoff, a reaction beat after
  a shock, a moment that shows the relationship before it is tested.
- **Change** an event only when the story is broken without it; explain what breaks and why the change
  preserves the original idea. Mark it `PROPOSAL:` if the story was approved.

Every decision goes in the changes table: element · decision · reason · impact on other scenes.

## 3. Pacing tools
- **Enter late, leave early:** start a scene at the last possible moment, end it as soon as it turns.
- **Vary scene length:** a long tense scene followed by a short sharp one.
- **Escalate:** each obstacle bigger or more personal than the last.
- **Compress time** with a cut, a montage, or a sound bridge rather than travel scenes.
- **Slow down** for the moments that matter (decisions, reveals, losses) — give them reactions and silence.
- **Ticking clock** only if it is real and visible.

## 4. Emotional moments
An emotional moment needs: a setup earlier (the audience must care), a pause before (anticipation), the
moment itself shown in behavior, and a reaction after. Look for places where the source rushes past a
feeling and give it one more beat.

## 5. AI-production risk list
Flag these in the scene card; keep them if the story needs them, and tell the director:
- Readable text, signs, screens, handwriting → `[[VFX: ...]]`.
- Crowds or many characters in physical contact (fights, dances, hugs of three or more).
- Fine hand actions (writing, tying, locks, instruments) longer than a moment.
- Fast costume changes or many costumes per character.
- Long continuous takes with complex movement through several spaces.
- Characters who must look identical (twins, clones) or must change age.
- Mirrors and reflections that must be accurate.
